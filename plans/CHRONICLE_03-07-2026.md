# The Jealous Age

> *"Live rightly, and the world shall be good."*
> — the founding creed of the Children of Breoroustosism, first faith of the world (day 15)

A single unbroken century, 5000 BC to 4900 BC, watched from birth to Medieval dusk. Forty souls became two thousand, and along the way they built 130 tribes, buried 162 dead religions, declared 860 wars that killed almost no one — and murdered 407 of their own out of love gone sour.

---

## Executive summary

- **The world grew twentyfold and survived.** Forty founders became a peak of **2,322** souls and closed at **2,017** — a genuine, self-sustaining civilization, not a collapse. (**13,463 births** against **11,481 deaths** over 4,950 days.)
- **This was a long-lived people.** Nearly **95% of all deaths (10,943 of 11,528) were old age.** Famine, plague, and squalor barely touched the death toll. The great killer of the young was not nature.
- **Jealousy was the engine of history.** The logs record **19,110 jealousy flare-ups**, which drove **979 marriages to collapse** ("trust collapsed under jealousy" — *every* recorded separation) and **4,553 crimes of passion**, of which **407 were lethal.**
- **Love killed 37 times more people than war.** All 860 declared wars and 1,157 battles produced just **11 war deaths.** The bedroom was deadlier than the battlefield by an order of magnitude.
- **Disease raged and lost.** Souls contracted plague, typhus, malaria, and fever **~68,700 times** — and were **cured 66,365 times.** Illness claimed only **24 lives.** Their bodies were far stronger than their hearts.
- **Faith was born to die.** **168 religions** flickered into being; **162 went extinct.** Only **6** survived to the end, led by the *Servants of Greothwosism*.
- **Knowledge crept, politics churned.** **29 technologies** endured, across **181 recorded dark ages** and **1,775 treaties** — a restless, fractious, endlessly re-aligning political map of **130 tribes**.

## Society & the emotional engine

If this civilization had a heart, it was a jealous one. The event stream makes the causal chain unmistakable:

> **19,110 jealousy events → 979 marriages dissolved + 4,553 crimes of passion → 407 killings.**

Every one of the **979 recorded separations** carries the same cause: *"trust collapsed under jealousy."* Against **1,884 couples formed**, more than half of all unions that ended, ended in suspicion. Of the 4,553 crimes of passion, **roughly 8.9% turned lethal** — a jealous rage killed about one time in eleven.

The victims were not the frail. Their mean age was **48** (range 14 to 82), and many died in the prime of a happy life. The very first murder in the record is emblematic: **Hysa**, age 29, partnered, her happiness reading a perfect **100** at the moment of death — killed by **Teoor**, one of the world's earliest-born children. Contentment offered no protection when someone else's jealousy found you.

Contrast this with war. The tribes were relentlessly political: **860 wars declared, 850 of them ethnic, 1,157 battles, 1,775 treaties signed, zero conquests.** Yet all that sabre-rattling produced **11 deaths.** War here was theater — posturing, alliance, and grievance (**368 blood-grievances** logged) that almost never spilled real blood. The knife between two lovers was, statistically, a soul's greatest mortal danger.

## Faith, tribes & knowledge

**Religion was a graveyard of gods.** The world generated **168 faiths** and buried **162** of them — a **96% extinction rate.** The first, **Children of Breoroustosism**, was founded by Moushwyshax on day 15 with the gentle motto *"Live rightly, and the world shall be good."* A hundred years later only **6** faiths remained, the largest being the **Servants of Greothwosism**. Belief here was abundant, cheap, and mortal.

**Tribes multiplied and fissured** — **446 founded** in all, **130** standing at the end. The very first, the **United Grouthteis**, was raised by Glyshglothuna on day 0; within five days the rival **Hard Peishteogreoth** had split off under Glothar, and the age of factions began. The political texture of the run is dense: **13,909 specialist appointments, 7,738 alliances, 2,784 rivalries, 2,740 migrations, 31 exiles.**

**Knowledge advanced slowly and unevenly**, through **181 dark ages** — repeated forgettings and relearnings. The first innovation was fittingly grim: a **"Death Rite,"** discovered on day 5. By the end, **29 technologies** had taken permanent root.

## Economy & environment

The land was mostly kind. Across the century the environment log records only **236 famines** against a backdrop of "ordinary" and "bountiful" years, and starvation appears nowhere among the causes of death. The economy sustained a specialist class — nearly **14,000 specialist roles** filled over time (scholars, traders, healers, craftsmen) — the surplus of a society whose bodies were rarely hungry. What it could not manufacture was emotional security.

## Notable lives

**Heosheth** (Entity 1) — *the anxious founder who lasted.* Born with the most extreme temperament of any founder (neuroticism **100**, openness **93**), he began the world stressed, lonely, and searching (*Goal: find_partner*). He found what he sought: he died at **68**, still partnered, having raised **7 children.** The most neurotic soul in creation built a family and saw it survive him.

**Tythglothor** (Entity 20616) — *the patriarch.* Died an elder at 78 with **34 recorded children**, the most fertile life in the entire history — a single man responsible for a measurable slice of the population curve.

**Hysa** (Entity 20058) — *the first casualty of love.* Age 29, in a committed partnership, happiness at its ceiling — and the world's first murder victim, killed by Teoor. Her death opened the ledger that jealousy would fill 406 more times.

**Glyshglothuna** (Entity 9) — *the first chief.* Founder of the United Grouthteis on day 0 and, five days later, discoverer of the world's first innovation, the Death Rite — the same soul who organized the first society also gave it its first way of honoring the dead.

## Closing reflection

This was not a civilization destroyed by its enemies, its climate, or its plagues — it beat all three. Its people lived long, bred prodigiously, healed from nearly every fever, and turned aside almost every war before it drew blood. What they could never master was each other. The single force that shaped this history more than any harvest or army was **jealousy** — the suspicion between lovers that dissolved a thousand marriages and, hundreds of times, ended in a body on the ground. They built tribes, gods, and tools by the hundred and watched most of them die; the one thing that never went extinct was the human capacity to be undone by love. For a hundred years, in a world that gave them every chance to survive, they mostly did — and the ones who didn't were, more often than not, killed by someone who once loved them.
